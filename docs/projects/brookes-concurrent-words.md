# Concurrent words in the Brookes SC model

Import `Silmaril.Semantics.Brookes.Word.Thms`. Supply an alphabet, its
independence relation `I`, and a command `letter a : Command Store` for each
letter. For the event/sync alphabet the adjacent swaps are exactly these.

| Pair | Swap condition |
| --- | --- |
| `event(t,e)`, `event(u,f)` | `t ≠ u` |
| `event(t,e)`, `sync(P)` in either order | `t ∉ P` |
| `sync(P)`, `sync(Q)` | Never, including disjoint participant sets |

The trace equivalence is generated by these swaps in arbitrary prefix and
suffix contexts. Its product is concatenation and its unit is the empty word.
`Word.Swap I` describes one contextual swap, and `Quot (Word.Swap I)` uses the
equivalence generated by this relation. The bridge works over an arbitrary
alphabet and has no dependency on a particular finite trace implementation.
[Definitions][defs] · [Theorems][thms] · [Zielonka, §2][zielonka-doi]

## Two interpretations

`Word.eval letter` folds a representative using `Command.skip` and
`Command.seq`. Its target is the existing `Behavior Store Unit` of nonempty
open `Trace Store` words. Each trace is a list of store pairs. At a join the
post-store of one segment and the pre-store of the next can differ.
`Command.seq` closes concatenated words under identity insertion and
contraction of matching transitions. Those deductions admit additional
observations and preserve the terminal value. The swap equation is a separate
obligation, expressed as equality of the resulting behaviors. [Brookes core][core]

- **Full open behavior.** `Word.Commutes I letter` requires
  `seq (letter a) (letter b) = seq (letter b) (letter a)` for every `I a b`.
  `eval_of_swap` extends each equation through both word contexts.
  `lift` interprets the quotient, `lift_mk` computes it on representatives,
  and `lift_append` preserves concatenation by `seq`. The empty representative
  maps to `skip` by `eval_nil`. `commutes_of_lift` recovers the local equations
  from any factorization by applying it to the two-letter representatives.
- **Contiguous endpoint relation.** `Word.endpoints c s t` is `c.completes s t ()`,
  membership of the singleton store pair `[(s,t)]`. `endpoints_skip` gives
  equality and `endpoints_seq` gives relational composition, including the
  existential intermediate store. `CommutesAtEndpoints` requires equality of
  the two composite relations for each swappable pair. It is sufficient for
  `liftEndpoints` and necessary by `commutesAtEndpoints_of_lift`.
  `liftEndpoints_mk` computes on representatives and `liftEndpoints_append`
  preserves concatenation by relational composition.

Each lift preserves and reflects its selected observation on every representative
by equality with that representative's interpretation. Semantic equality can
identify different trace classes. For example, if a sync has command `skip`, its
one-letter word has the same interpretation as the empty word, although every
swap preserves word length. The unit-result target needs a fixed read reply to
be checked by a letter's guard or retained in state if it is to affect later
observations. Theorems about endpoint equality apply in sequential contexts.
Brookes parallel uses a shuffle of whole open words. Brookes gives two
commands with the same standalone initial/final observations that a parallel
`await` context distinguishes. [Brookes, journal §7, p. 152][brookes-pdf] · [Brookes core][core]

## A sufficient condition and two obstructions

`product_commutes_at_endpoints` applies when the store splits as `X × Y` and
the two endpoint relations have the forms

```
p ((x,y), (x',y'))  iff  L x x' ∧ y = y'
q ((x,y), (x',y'))  iff  x = x' ∧ R y y'
```

Both orders then reduce to `L x x' ∧ R y y'`. The local relations may be
partial or nondeterministic. The split constrains enabling conditions as well
as writes. For an event and a sync, `X` can be the event's private state and
`Y` can contain the participants' state. Any scheduler state, shared memory,
barrier counters, or observation log must also satisfy the two factor laws
for that pair. Participant membership alone supplies only the syntactic swap
rule. Zielonka's global states and local transition functions use the same
product argument. His model takes disjoint domains to induce independence,
whereas the specified alphabet still orders every sync/sync pair. [Zielonka, §4 and Lemma 4.1][zielonka-doi]

- **Shared cell.** The regression interprets two distinct thread letters by
  actual `TreeSC.writeBehavior` commands, writing `1` and `2` to the sole
  location. From `0` only the order `write 1; write 2` can finish at `2`.
  The syntactic swap identifies these words, so there is no endpoint lift
  for this interpretation, and hence no open lift. A read label asserting
  reply `0` on the same location also distinguishes its position relative to
  a write of `1` from that initial store. SC permits either cross-thread
  order that preserves each thread's own order. The outcomes may differ.
  [Lean regression][regression] · [Lamport, p. 690][lamport-doi]
- **Disjoint writes.** Starting at `(false,false)`, setting the left and
  right bits to `true` yields the same endpoints in either order. The open
  word with intermediate store `(true,false)` belongs only to the left-first
  command. If the reverse command accepted it, `Runs.of_reduces` would force
  a contiguous reverse seed with intermediate store `(false,true)`. Every
  transition of that seed preserves the predicate `store = (true,false)`
  from pre-store to post-store. Stuttering and mumbling preserve this property,
  but the observed second transition violates it. The product criterion
  therefore establishes an endpoint lift even for examples that refute an
  open lift. [Lean regression][regression]

## Connection to the existing APIs

- `TreeSC.Frame.transition` forgets the exchange label and reply and retains
  its two stores. `TreeSC.treeBehavior_code` identifies the open tree semantics
  with the existing recursive denotation over `readBehavior`, `writeBehavior`,
  and bind. `TreeSC.denote_completes_iff_sc` concerns the singleton SC scheduler
  with an existential event list. An event/sync adapter needs its own
  correspondence between each letter command and its intended scheduler step,
  including the effect and enabling conditions of a sync. [TreeSC completed behaviors][tree]
- `StoreEquiv` transports full words in both directions along a store
  equivalence. Its `Behavior.transport_ret` and `Behavior.transport_bind` give
  compatibility with the word fold, and `Behavior.transport_completes_iff`
  transports the endpoint criterion. Changing representation preserves and
  reflects the selected observations. An arbitrary map can identify the two
  sides of a genuine store gap. [StoreEquiv theorems][store-equiv]
- `Behavior.iterate` unions the finite approximants of a result-dependent
  body. An observation comes with a finite bound by `iterate_accepts_iff`.
  The word bridge deals with a supplied finite completed schedule. To interpret
  a loop by these words, relate its completed approximants to the schedule
  interpretation, including the state and result used to select the next call.
  The bridge supplies no observation of infinite schedules or fairness. [Brookes iteration][iteration]

Once the finite trace API is available, an adapter under
`Silmaril/Semantics/Brookes/Word/EventSync/` can instantiate `I` by the table
and use the source quotient's lift with `Word.eval_of_swap` or
`Word.endpoints_of_swap` on each generator. The adapter's computation theorem
should state `denote (classOf w) = Word.eval letter w`, or
`observe (classOf w) = Word.endpoints (Word.eval letter w)` for the endpoint
version. These equations and `Word.eval_append` give the unit and product laws
using the source API's representative multiplication. A scheduler adequacy
claim additionally needs the per-letter correspondence and the appropriate
swap hypothesis on the resulting commands.

## Sources

- Stephen Brookes, *Full Abstraction for a Shared-Variable Parallel Language*,
  LICS 1993, §5, and *Information and Computation* 127(2), 145–163, 1996,
  §6 and Propositions 6.1–6.2. The finite semantics describes external
  interference between transitions, singleton observations, and closed
  concatenation and interleaving. [LICS manuscript][lics] · [Journal DOI][brookes-doi] · [Journal scan][brookes-pdf]
- Yotam Dvir, Ohad Kammar, Ori Lahav and Gordon D. Plotkin,
  *Two-sorted algebraic decompositions of Brookes’s shared-state denotational
  semantics*, FoSSaCS 2025, 377–398, §6.1. This is the return-bearing carrier
  and the single-sorted closure and bind used here. [DOI][dvir-doi] · [arXiv v3][dvir]
- Pierre Cartier and Dominique Foata, *Problèmes combinatoires de commutation
  et réarrangements*, LNM 85, 1969, and Antoni Mazurkiewicz,
  *Introduction to Trace Theory*, *The Book of Traces*, 3–41, 1995.
  The quotient uses the free partially commutative monoid presentation.
  [Cartier–Foata][cartier] · [Mazurkiewicz][mazurkiewicz]
- Wiesław Zielonka, *Notes on finite asynchronous automata*, RAIRO ITA 21(2),
  99–135, 1987, §§2 and 4. The trace congruence and the disjoint-domain
  execution lemma supply the syntactic and operational sides of the comparison.
  [DOI][zielonka-doi] · [PDF][zielonka-pdf]
- Leslie Lamport, *How to Make a Multiprocessor Computer That Correctly
  Executes Multiprocess Programs*, IEEE Transactions on Computers C-28(9),
  690–691, 1979. The SC
  definition fixes the order per processor and requires a sequential order
  for the execution as a whole. [DOI][lamport-doi] · [Author’s scan][lamport-pdf]
