# Finite concurrent traces

Import `Silmaril.Semantics.ConcurrentTrace.Thms`. A `Word Thread Event` is a
finite list of `Letter.event thread event` and `Letter.sync participants`
letters. An event can swap with an event on a different thread or with a
synchronization whose list omits its thread. Distinct synchronizations retain
their order, including when their participant lists are disjoint or empty.
The lists are literal, so order and duplicates within a list are preserved.
[Definitions][defs] · [Theorems][thms]

## Composition and maps

- `Equivalent` is the least concatenation congruence generated by the swaps.
  `ofWord` sends a word to its class in the monoid `Trace`. The monoid product
  appends representatives. `lift` and `hom_ext` give the universal property for
  monoid interpretations of the letters that respect independence.
- `saturate` is the preimage of the quotient image. `languageEquiv` identifies
  saturated word languages with arbitrary sets of traces, ordered by inclusion.
  `concat` saturates pointwise concatenation and corresponds to pointwise
  multiplication of trace sets. The singleton empty word is a unit on
  saturated word languages.
- `mapTrace` takes an injective thread function and any event function, with
  `rename` and `mapEvents` for mapping one field. The domain can be a subtype
  of thread IDs. The maps preserve identity, composition and the quotient
  image of word languages. Saturating the mapped words recovers all target
  representatives. With a nonempty event type, `injective_of_respects` proves
  injectivity is necessary for a map defined on all words of its source type.
  Collapsing two IDs makes a formerly independent event and one-thread sync
  dependent, regardless of how event payloads are mapped.

## Observations

`liftThread t` puts a sequential event word on thread `t`. `observe t`
retains its events and all participating synchronizations with their full
participant lists. `events t` erases the synchronization markers and is a
left inverse of `liftThread t`. `syncs` retains all synchronizations in order,
including empty participant lists. These monoid observations commute with
the corresponding thread and event maps. `swap_eq_iff` uses the local and
global observations to characterize commutation of two singleton traces.

## Threaded computations and prefix ideals

Import [`Silmaril.Semantics.ConcurrentTrace.EventSync.Thms`][event-sync]
for the threaded state and nondeterminism instances over this alphabet.
`quotientOutput` sends free-monoid output to `Trace`. Its action on
allocative computations commutes with bind and with output relabelling.
`completeEquiv` identifies computations with finite trace output with the
raw computations that contain all output representatives at each fixed
result and final used-name set.

`wordMap` equips the finite trace monoid with the prefix order given by
left divisibility. `includeFinite` embeds a finite class with optional
completion as a principal ideal with that completion. In particular, the
raw completed branch and the branch routed through the finite quotient
give the same ideal observation, with both the result and the final used
set retained. `mapPrefix_includeFinite` commutes with injective thread
maps, arbitrary event maps and result maps. On the whole ideal quotient,
`observe_mapPrefix` describes this action by taking the downward closure
of the image of an ideal.

The output relabelling square holds at the same current thread and used-name
input. A binder-level alpha quotient still needs a proof that changing
fresh-name representatives respects dependent sequencing, with the
stabilizer updated to account for the continuation's used names.
The completed-branch comparison and the [ideal model][ideals] leave
divergence-observing nondeterministic bind and the complete Elgot laws open.
The [Brookes adapter][brookes] treats finite open behaviors and contiguous
endpoints under their respective descent hypotheses. A VM connection would
require an event/sync labelling of VM steps and a proof relating those
steps and their observations to the selected trace interpretation.

The trace monoid uses the partially commutative monoid construction of Pierre
Cartier and Dominique Foata, *Problèmes combinatoires de commutation et
réarrangements*, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 85 (1969), Chapter I §2 and
Lemma 1.1. The July 2006 electronic reedition PDF has SHA-256
`19996a8b31579bbac8cae5bba12a33235727e002635adf213f0e511d63508fbc`.
[DOI][doi] · [Reedition PDF][pdf]
